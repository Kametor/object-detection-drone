02 / 16
Problem

Detect and localize one object class in unlabeled drone footage

The real-world shape of this problem

Large video archives arrive with no ground truth. Manual labeling at scale is expensive and slow — the practical question isn't "which detector is best," it's how much of the labeling can a model do for itself, and how do you know when to trust it.

What this study had to produce

  • A labeling strategy under a real label budget
  • A simple baseline, and its actual failure mode
  • A genuinely different alternative — not a re-tuned copy
  • Quantitative and qualitative comparison, honestly reported

Data & Target

13 videos, 6 usable, one class chosen by evidence

Footage spans Swiss Alps and Lake Geneva, Scotland (Glencoe), and at least one non-European trail — not the single-region set it first appeared to be. person was chosen after visual triage: present in 6/13 videos, 500+ instances, real scale variation and hard negatives (vegetation occlusion, distant figures against rock and shadow, people beside sled dogs in snow).

The problem this baseline exposes

At any confidence threshold, plain YOLO finds zero of 44 small/distant people on the hardest two test videos — not a threshold problem, a feature-map resolution problem. This single finding is what everything else in this study responds to.

06 / 16
Alternative 1 — Detect-then-Verify Cascade

Lower YOLO's confidence, then let a second model clean up

Same YOLO26n, floor dropped to conf=0.01 to force it to propose the small-object boxes it was silently discarding — then a trained ResNet18 crop classifier re-examines every ambiguous box.

✕
Verify everything, including confident boxesF1 0.514 — the verifier wrongly rejected boxes YOLO already had right.
✓
Fix: only verify the ambiguous band [0.01, 0.25)mAP@.5 → 0.760. Diagnosed why the verifier was weak: one video (DJI_0790) was 70% of its training crops.
✓
Video-balanced trainingEvery source video weighted equally per epoch. Small-object recall goes from exactly zero to real: 10 of 44 previously-invisible people caught.
✓
Score fusion + 3×3 "small-input" ResNet stemReplace the hard accept/reject with a blended confidence; swap ResNet's 7×7 stem for 3×3 (CIFAR-style) so 64×64 crops keep their detail. Final: mAP@.5 0.788, F1 0.791.

Two ideas tried and dropped, on purpose

Narrowing the confidence band — expected an easier task for the verifier; instead lost real signal in the 0.01–0.10 range. mAP fell.
Size-based gating (always verify small boxes) — the rare high-confidence small detections YOLO does produce turned out reliable; asking a fallible verifier to re-check them was pure downside.

Every one of these is a logged, dated decision with numbers — not a clean narrative written after the fact.

What the cascade is actually for

Not beating the baseline's headline F1 — recovering exactly the failure mode Method 1 exposed. 10 of 44 previously-invisible small people, caught.

Architecture aside worth keeping

Swapping ResNet's 7×7 stem for 3×3 improved every metric on this task's 64×64 crops — a one-line change, isolated and confirmed on both CPU and T4.

Forcing YOLO's resolution onto RT-DETR broke it

Pushed both checkpoints from their native 640×640 to YOLO's 1920×1088, expecting a "fairer" comparison.

0.740→0.253
RT-DETRv2-R18 mAP@.5 — collapse
0.731→0.654
RT-DETR-l — degraded, not collapsed

Root cause: RT-DETR has no NMS at inference — its one-query-per-object behavior is learned for one specific resolution. YOLO's CNN+NMS pipeline tolerates the same resolution change with no retraining at all. A real, checkpoint-dependent fragility — not a bug in our pipeline.

Alternative 3 — SAM3, Zero-Shot Foundation Model

The genuinely different approach: no training at all

Every method so far is a trained or fine-tunable supervised detector. SAM3 is prompted directly with the word "person" — zero exposure to this project's data.

The clearest paradigm trade-off in the study: best possible accuracy with zero training, at a real cost in precision and a severe cost in speed. Consistent with its actual role here — a zero-shot ceiling-finder and pseudo-labeling teacher, not a deployment candidate.

Fewer parameters ≠ faster, off-GPU

EfficientNet-B0 has 64% fewer parameters than ResNet18, yet ran ~5× slower per crop on CPU — depthwise-separable convolutions vectorize poorly on general CPU kernels; the efficiency gain is real, but GPU-side.

Strengths & Weaknesses

No single method wins on every axis

●YOLO26n (baseline)

  • Fastest, simplest, zero training cost
  • Best precision of any method tried
  • Structurally blind to small/distant people

●Cascade (ResNet/EfficientNet)

  • Only method that recovers small-object recall from zero
  • Two-stage cost; verifier generalization is data-hungry
  • Best mAP among trained, deployable methods

●RT-DETR (transformer)

  • Genuinely different architecture family, own strengths
  • Fragile to inference resolution — no NMS to fall back on
  • Fine-tuning not attempted this cycle (time-boxed, see notes)

●SAM3 (foundation model)

  • Best accuracy and recall, zero training
  • ~10× slower than any trained detector — not deployable as-is
  • Ideal as a labeling teacher, which is exactly how it's used here

Future Work

Time-boxed out of this cycle, not out of the plan

Train YOLO26s on our own pseudo-labels

Every result so far builds on zero-shot YOLO. Actually fine-tuning on this project's SAM3 pseudo-labels is the highest-priority remaining item.

TensorRT export + FP16

Current FPS numbers understate deployable speed considerably; a quantized/compiled export is the standard next step.

YOLO P2 head + SAHI tiling

Both target exactly this study's core failure mode (small objects) directly in the detector, rather than via a second-stage verifier.

ConvNeXt + CenterNet + FPN

A fifth architecture family, planned as a lower-priority Tier 2 comparison.

Train on a larger public UAV dataset (UAV-VisLoc)

An illustrative "what if the label budget were larger" data point, not a core deliverable.

Synthetic data & edge quantization

Blender domain-randomized augmentation and INT8 deployment — genuine directions, explicitly out of scope for a 7-day cycle.

16 / 16
Closing

The honest version of the story

A simple baseline exposed a specific, real failure. Three genuinely different responses to it — a second-stage verifier, a transformer architecture, a zero-shot foundation model — each traded accuracy against speed, precision against recall, in a different place. None of them is a strictly better replacement for the others; each is documented with the experiment that produced it, including the ones that didn't work.
